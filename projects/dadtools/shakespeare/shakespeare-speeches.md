# Famous Shakespeare Speeches, Monologues & Sonnets

Grouped by play, in no particular order.

## Speeches & Monologues

### Hamlet
- "O, that this too too solid flesh would melt": Hamlet, 1.2
- "Neither a borrower nor a lender be": Polonius's advice to Laertes, 1.3
- "Something is rotten in the state of Denmark": Marcellus, 1.4
- "There are more things in heaven and earth, Horatio": Hamlet, 1.5
- "The time is out of joint": Hamlet, 1.5
- "Brevity is the soul of wit": Polonius, 2.2
- "There is nothing either good or bad but thinking makes it so": Hamlet, 2.2
- "What a piece of work is a man": Hamlet, 2.2
- "O, what a rogue and peasant slave am I": Hamlet, 2.2
- "To be, or not to be": Hamlet, 3.1
- "Get thee to a nunnery": Hamlet, 3.1
- "O, what a noble mind is here o'erthrown!": Ophelia, 3.1
- "Speak the speech, I pray you": advice to the players, 3.2
- "The lady doth protest too much, methinks": Gertrude, 3.2
- "O, my offence is rank": Claudius at prayer, 3.3
- "How all occasions do inform against me": Hamlet, 4.4 (in the Second Quarto only)
- "There's rosemary, that's for remembrance": Ophelia's flowers, 4.5
- "There is a willow grows aslant a brook": Gertrude on Ophelia's death, 4.7
- "Alas, poor Yorick": Hamlet, 5.1
- "The readiness is all": Hamlet, 5.2
- "The rest is silence… Good night, sweet prince": Hamlet and Horatio, 5.2

### Macbeth
- "When shall we three meet again… Fair is foul, and foul is fair": the Witches, 1.1
- "The raven himself is hoarse… Come, you spirits": Lady Macbeth, 1.5
- "If it were done when 'tis done": Macbeth, 1.7
- "Is this a dagger which I see before me": Macbeth, 2.1
- "Sleep no more! Macbeth does murder sleep": Macbeth, 2.2
- "Will all great Neptune's ocean wash this blood": Macbeth, 2.2
- "Here's a knocking indeed!": the Porter, 2.3
- "Double, double toil and trouble": the Witches, 4.1
- "By the pricking of my thumbs, / Something wicked this way comes": Second Witch, 4.1
- "Out, damned spot": Lady Macbeth sleepwalking, 5.1
- "Tomorrow, and tomorrow, and tomorrow": Macbeth, 5.5
- "Lay on, Macduff": Macbeth, 5.8

### Julius Caesar
- "Why, man, he doth bestride the narrow world / Like a Colossus": Cassius, 1.2
- "Yond Cassius has a lean and hungry look": Caesar, 1.2
- "Cowards die many times before their deaths": Caesar, 2.2
- "Et tu, Brute? Then fall, Caesar": Caesar, 3.1
- "O, pardon me, thou bleeding piece of earth… Cry 'Havoc!'": Antony, 3.1
- "Romans, countrymen, and lovers": Brutus's funeral oration, 3.2
- "Friends, Romans, countrymen": Antony's funeral oration, 3.2
- "There is a tide in the affairs of men": Brutus, 4.3
- "This was the noblest Roman of them all": Antony, 5.5

### Romeo and Juliet
- Queen Mab: Mercutio, 1.4
- "O, she doth teach the torches to burn bright!": Romeo, 1.5
- "But soft, what light through yonder window breaks": Romeo, 2.2
- "O Romeo, Romeo, wherefore art thou Romeo?": Juliet, 2.2
- "Parting is such sweet sorrow": Juliet, 2.2
- "These violent delights have violent ends": Friar Lawrence, 2.6
- "A plague o' both your houses!": Mercutio, 3.1
- "Gallop apace, you fiery-footed steeds": Juliet, 3.2
- The potion speech, "I have a faint cold fear thrills through my veins": Juliet, 4.3
- "O happy dagger, / This is thy sheath": Juliet, 5.3

### King Lear

- "I am a man / More sinned against than sinning": Lear, 3.2
- "Poor naked wretches": Lear, 3.4
- "As flies to wanton boys are we to th' gods": Gloucester, 4.1

- "Come, let's away to prison": Lear, 5.3
- "Howl, howl, howl, howl!": Lear, 5.3
